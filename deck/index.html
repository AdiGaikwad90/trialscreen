TrialScreen

Eligibility screening that shows its working.

A working prototype for clinical trial patient recruitment — and for the reason every previous attempt stalled.

Synthetic patient records throughout. No real patient data is used anywhere in this demo.

Recruitment is where trials stall.

A single protocol carries 20 to 50 eligibility criteria. Today a coordinator opens one chart at a time and checks it against every one of them, by hand.

Most trials miss their enrolment timeline, and recruitment is the largest single reason why. Every week of delay is a week the drug is not reaching anyone.

Three people have to say yes.

Uses it daily

Clinical research coordinator

Screens the cohort and works the review queue. Today that is weeks of chart review, and the part of the job most likely to be done under time pressure.

Pays for it

Trial lead, clinical operations

Owns the enrolment timeline and the budget it consumes. Needs to know which criterion is costing the most enrolment, not just how many patients are left.

Can veto it

Quality and regulatory

Must be able to reconstruct any screening decision, months later, from the record. If that is not possible, the tool does not get used.

A tool that satisfies only the first two never ships. That is the whole design constraint.

No audit trail

A regulator cannot inspect a probability. Screening decisions have to be reproducible from the record, years after the fact.

No clinical trust

An investigator will not enrol a patient on reasoning they are not allowed to check — and should not.

So it stays a pilot

The models work. They just never make it out of the evaluation phase, because nobody can sign the result.

So we split the job in three.

AI

Reads the protocol

Turns written criteria into structured rules a human approves before anyone is screened.

NO AI

The rule engine decides

Pure TypeScript. No network, no randomness, no clock. The same inputs always give the same answer.

AI

Writes it up

A short note in plain language, composed only from the results the engine produced.

The model never decides who is eligible.

It translates English into rules at the front, and results into English at the back. Every verdict in between is arithmetic you can re-derive by hand.

How it is built.

CLOUDFLARE WORKER

Next.js 16 · React 19 · TypeScript

Server-rendered at the edge through OpenNext. Sessions are signed cookies; roles are checked server-side on every request.

TRUST BOUNDARY — NO AI INSIDE

The rule engine

Pure functions over the patient record. Deterministic, covered by 29 tests, and the only thing in the system that produces a verdict.

CLOUDFLARE

Workers AI

Llama 3.3 70B in JSON-schema mode. Reads the protocol and writes the notes. Never decides.

CLOUDFLARE

D1

Every screening run and every reviewer decision, stored against a name and a timestamp.

One platform, no servers to run, and nothing in the stack that bills per seat while it sits idle.

What the prototype proves.

114msto screen 50 patients against 9 criteria
29tests over the rule engine, all passing
0real patient records, anywhere in the system

Reading every criterion twice caught two real defects

✗
An exclusion read backwards “Haemoglobin below 10 g/dL” became hgb ≤ 10. It would have excluded 49 of 50 otherwise-eligible patients — silently.
✗
A dropped duration “for at least 5 years” quietly became a presence check, admitting patients the protocol excludes.

Both were caught because every criterion is read twice and a human is shown the disagreement. That is the product, demonstrating itself.
